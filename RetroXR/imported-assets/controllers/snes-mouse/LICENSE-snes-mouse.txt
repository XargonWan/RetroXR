MODIFICATIONS (CC-BY-4.0 requires these be indicated)

Adapted for RetroXR as snes_mouse.glb and snes_mouse_plug.glb.

  * Removed every Nintendo wordmark. Unlike the trademarks on other imported
    hardware, none of these is a mesh — they live in the TEXTURES, and in three
    different maps, so each had to be found and painted out separately:

      - the shell's BASE COLOUR (snes_mouse.glb): the printed wordmark, a
        139 x 34 px region of the atlas;
      - the shell's OCCLUSION + metallicRoughness atlas: the underside label's
        moulded pill logo and its "(c) 1992 NINTENDO" line, carried as relief;
      - the plug's NORMAL map and its OCCLUSION + metallicRoughness map: the
        pill logo moulded into the connector's grip.

    Each region is filled with the median of a ring just outside it, so a normal
    map gets its flat normal back and an AO plate its plate value. The rest of
    the underside label is untouched, none of it being a mark: "MADE IN JAPAN",
    "PAT. PENDING", "RATING: DC6V 45mA", "MODEL NO. SNS-016".

    Verified by decoding the images out of each shipped .glb's own binary chunk,
    resolving material -> texture -> image, and confirming every cleaned region
    is flat. That check matters twice over: a base-colour render cannot show a
    mark that only exists as relief, which is how the last four survived a pass
    that reported the model clean.

  * Removed the mouse pad entirely (not wanted, and it carried a second
    Nintendo wordmark of its own).

  * Removed the cable. The source builds it from a spline and it is 22,400 of
    the model's 28,251 triangles; RetroXR simulates every cord with a VerletRope
    instead. The plug on its end is kept and exported separately.

  * Split the connector into snes_mouse_plug.glb, authored to RetroXR's plug
    contract: D-shaped shroud on +Z, ribbed strain relief trailing -Z, origin on
    the shoulder where the shroud meets the grip — the face that meets a
    console's port cheek. Its frame comes from the plug's own principal axes;
    aligning it to the cable's local direction instead put a roll and a tilt
    into it (the tell was a 42.6 x 37.2 mm bounding box for a body whose true
    section is 42.7 x 17.0 mm).

  * Un-posed the mouse. The source is a "trophy pose" — the shell is rotated in
    the scene — so it was axis-aligned into the shell's own frame and re-seated
    flat, front (buttons and cord) on -Z per retro_mouse.tscn's convention.

  * Parts named for RetroXR: BodyMesh, LeftButton, RightButton, Ball, Inner,
    Underside. Transforms baked into the vertex data.

NOT modified: the model is already at real-world scale (61 x 37 x 96 mm against
a real SNES mouse's ~58 x 35 x 100 mm), so unlike other imports no scale
normalisation was applied.

MODIFICATIONS (CC-BY-4.0 requires these be indicated)

Adapted for RetroXR as atari_2600_cx40.glb — the CX40 joystick body.

  * Removed the Atari trademark moulded into its top face (a separate mesh).

  * Scaled by 0.7881 to match the console, and rotation/scale baked into the
    vertex data while each part's own origin was preserved: those origins are
    the pivots the stick and fire button rotate and press about.

  * The stick, its rubber boot and the fire button are kept as separate objects
    so the pad can animate; the rest of the shell is joined into one mesh.

Adapted for RetroXR as atari_2600_plug_cx40.glb and atari_2600_plug_paddle.glb
— the two controller plug ends, taken from the loose cables in the source scene.

  * Removed the Atari trademark from both. The source moulds one onto each plug
    body; they are separate meshes and were deleted outright.

  * Scaled by 0.7881 to match the console, which is normalised to real-world
    size. The source asset is uniformly ~1.27x oversized.

  * Reoriented to RetroXR's plug contract: connector on +Z, cable trailing -Z,
    with the origin at the mating tip so a port snap zone lines up with it.
    Authored so the exporter's Z-up to Y-up conversion LANDS on that contract
    rather than being applied before it.

  * Transforms baked into the vertex data. Left on the node transforms, Godot
    sizes the part from the bounding box of a ROTATED box, over-measures it and
    shrinks the result.

  * Shell meshes joined and re-centred. Neither file embeds any image.

The two plugs are deliberately kept separate: the paddle plug is 3 mm longer and
carries a small moulded text panel the joystick plug does not have.

Scenes/Objects/atari_2600_plug_{cx40,paddle}.res are ArrayMesh resources
baked from these GLBs, for ControllerPlug.set_plug_mesh().

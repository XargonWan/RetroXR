lamp/chain_pull.wav
    Cut from "Light Switch Chain.aif" by ftpalad — Freesound
    https://freesound.org/people/ftpalad/sounds/119924/
    CC0 1.0 (public domain). Original is 3.90 s, 48 kHz, 24-bit stereo — though
    the two channels are identical (L/R correlation 1.000), so it is flattened to
    mono with no loss.

    The 0.44-1.05 s region: the switch snapping over centre plus the chain
    recoiling afterwards. ONE clip serves both directions, because a pull-chain
    switch is the same detent going over centre either way and genuinely sounds
    the same on as off.

    Normalised x5.1 to peak 0.600 — the source peaks at only 0.118. That lifts
    the noise floor with it, from -62 dBFS to roughly 44 dB below the clip's own
    peak, which is inaudible at the level this plays at. A 3 ms fade at each end
    stops the cut boundary clicking.

    Rejected from the same take: the 2.36-2.98 s region, cut as a separate,
    softer "off". It needed x12.9 to match level, which would have lifted its
    hiss 8 dB more than the on's, and the mechanism does not actually sound
    different in the two directions anyway.

crt/crt_idle.wav, crt/crt_power_on.wav, crt/crt_power_off.wav
    Synthesised by Tools/gen_crt_hum.py from the physics of a CRT set: flyback
    whine at the 15734.264 Hz NTSC line rate, transformer buzz at twice mains,
    broadband hiss, and a degauss thunk at switch-on. Not sampled — every "CRT
    hum" recording found was unlicensed, personal-use-only (BBC RemArc), or
    behind a login with per-file terms.

    The idle loop is exactly periodic by construction, so it wraps seamlessly
    with no crossfade. The transients are built from the same recipe as the loop
    they hand off to, so the tail of the power-on matches the loop's RMS to
    within 0.2%.


=== Recorded in-house — no licence attaches =====================================

nes/nes_power_on_*.wav, nes_power_off_*.wav, nes_reset_press_*.wav,
nes/nes_reset_release_*.wav, nes_channel_switch_*.wav,
nes/nes_pad_face_press_*.wav, nes_pad_face_release_*.wav,
nes/nes_pad_menu_press_*.wav, nes_pad_dpad_press_*.wav, nes_pad_dpad_release_*.wav,
nes/nes_cart_insert_*.wav, nes_cart_remove_*.wav,
nes/nes_plug_in_*.wav, nes_plug_unplug_*.wav,
nes/nes_tray_down_*.wav, nes_tray_up_*.wav

    Original recordings of our own NES-001 console and pad. Nobody else's
    material is in them, so nothing has to be cleared to ship them.

    TWO microphones at once, because neither is usable alone. A Korg CM-300
    piezo contact mic on the shell hears the mechanism but almost nothing below
    500 Hz — into a PC mic jack's ~2 kΩ input a piezo's capacitance forms a
    high-pass, so it is all tick and no weight. An iPhone 15 Pro 10 cm away
    (Voice Memos, 24-bit/48 kHz, MONO — stereo would comb-filter when summed for
    a mono spatial emitter) supplies the body the contact mic cannot reach.

    The blend is inverted between the two sets, and deliberately so. For the
    CONSOLE the phone leads and the contact mic adds the attack above 1.5 kHz.
    For the PAD the contact mic leads, because it is mounted centimetres from the
    button and has real body there, while the phone is straining to hear a
    D-pad click at all — 17 dB down on it in the 150-1000 Hz band.

    Two measurement traps, both of which silently corrupt levels:

      * Voice Memos applies AGC that cannot be disabled. Measured at +1.97 dB/s
        — 22 dB across one 16 s take — with its noise floor climbing 19 dB while
        the contact mic's stayed flat. Every gesture is therefore corrected
        against the CONTACT mic, whose chain is fixed-gain analogue and cannot
        drift. Skip that and the "press-force variation" you preserve is mostly
        the phone.
      * A PC fan is airborne, so it lands only on the phone: +6 dB below 1 kHz,
        exactly the band the phone layer exists to supply. Removed by spectral
        subtraction against a profile taken from the take's own quietest 0.6 s,
        which drops that band's floor from -80.9 to -99.4 dBFS.

    Levels are matched per FAMILY, not per file, so hard and soft presses stay
    different; the spread is compressed to about 9 dB so no variant vanishes.

    Three pad banks, not eight buttons: A and B are the same moulding and sound
    identical, as do SELECT and START, as do the four arms of the rocker. Pooling
    them gives each bank far more variation than a per-button split would.

    There is no nes_pad_menu_release. SELECT and START have a long, soft return
    that never clears the noise floor — confirmed across two independent takes
    (29% and 30% short gaps, against 52-62% for the buttons that do have one).

    The cart and plug clips are cut to START AT THEIR PEAK, keeping 400 ms of
    decay. In the room a cartridge seats the instant the snap zone catches it,
    but a recording of a real hand approaches for half a second first — measured,
    the loudest moment sat 40-50% into every file, so the click arrived half a
    second after the visual and read as lag. The front half is the approach, not
    silence to be trimmed; dropping it is what makes the sound land on the event.

    The tray clips are NOT cut that way and should not be: a mechanism with a
    fixed travel and a hard stop already starts where it means to. That take is
    also the most consistent of the set (3.5-5.9 dB spread, against 11-21 dB for
    anything held in a hand), which is what a fixed travel buys you.


=== Import settings are load-bearing ============================================

Every .wav here MUST import with compress/mode=0 (uncompressed PCM). The
importer defaults to QOA, and SpatialAudioEmitter is PCM-push only — PcmClip
decodes to raw frames and refuses anything compressed, so a QOA clip loads as
ZERO frames and plays silence. .gitignore carries an exception so these .import
files ship.
